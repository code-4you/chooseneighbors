Transportable homes · overview

A room you can truck in and unfold in a day

What the cheap "expander" buildings and cabins from Express Portables actually are, how they arrive, what you get for the money, and where they sit with the council — written for the salvage / land-trust plan.

A transportable home is a small steel-framed building made in a factory and delivered to your site whole. The cheapest ones split into two ideas: cabins and pods that arrive as a finished box, and "expanders" that travel as a flat container and fold out on site into two or three times their travelling footprint. Both are the ready-made cousin of building a room from salvaged parts: faster and predictable, but you pay for the factory doing the work.

1The two kinds you'll see

Cabins & pods

from ~$9,950 (3 m pod)

A solid finished room — steel frame, insulated walls, a door, windows, lights and power points. 3 m, 4 m and 6 m sizes; some come with a bathroom or kitchenette. Good as a single bedroom, office, or sleep-out.

Expander (fold-out) homes

from ~$17,900 (24 m²)

Ships as a flat-pack container, then the walls and roof fold out with gas struts to make a much bigger space — the 24 m² unit opens to roughly 4 m × 6 m. More floor area per delivery, but the front panels need hinging on site (3+ people).

2How one actually arrives

  1. Order & build. You choose a model and options (cladding, air-con, hot water); the supplier quotes 12–15 weeks to build.
  2. Freight. It travels on a crane truck or tilt-tray from the Gold Coast factory. You need 10–15 m of straight, firm access for the truck to unload and drive away.
  3. Set down. The truck cranes or slides it onto a level base or piers you've prepared. Expanders are then unfolded and the front panels hinged.
  4. Connect. A licensed electrician hooks up the power and a plumber connects water and waste — these are not done for you.
  5. Move in. Once it's connected and signed off, it's a dry, lockable, insulated room.

3The council question — read this first

Building vs movable dwelling

Most of these are sold as portable / relocatable buildings and do not meet the Class 1a house standard in the Building Code by themselves. The supplier notes that when a unit is bought on one of their trailers, it is treated as a caravan (movable dwelling), which in many council areas can be placed without the same building approval.

What you may lawfully put on a block — and for how long someone can live in it — is decided by your local council and the zoning of the land, not by the supplier. There are also Class 1a expander models if you specifically need a code-compliant house. Always confirm with the council for your site before buying.

4What you pay for beyond the sticker

The advertised price is the building only, ex-factory. To live in one you also budget for delivery, a licensed electrician and plumber, footings or a base, and — if you want the movable-dwelling route — a trailer (about $13,950 for the 24 m² dual-axle). Optional extras like air-conditioning, hot water and cladding add roughly $800–$1,400 each. The 2026 price list works a landed-in-SA figure for each of the cheapest models.

5Where they fit the plan

Ready-made rooms beside the self-builds

The salvage plan has people building their own rooms from recycled parts on charity-held land. That's the cheapest path but the slowest, and not everyone in crisis can build. A handful of these transportable units are the fast complement: drop in two or three finished cabins so people are housed and the place looks lived-in within weeks, while self-builders get going alongside. It's the same "visible, occupied homes first" idea behind the funding case — just bought instead of built.

Honest notes

  • These are small. A 3 m–6 m cabin is one room. The 24 m² and 35 m² expanders are bigger but still compact; layouts beyond an open plan are mostly DIY.
  • "Move-in ready" has caveats. The building is supplied; the connection work (electrical fit-off, plumbing), footings and approvals are yours to arrange and pay for.
  • Specs and prices change. Models, options and discounts are set by the supplier and move often — this overview reflects the site as read on 19 June 2026.

MahanaCoromandel Peninsula, New Zealand

FadingWHY-RANK 001/219
SURVIVAL25
REASON INTEREST100
BRING-YOUR-OWN-VISION90
HOMOPHILY25
About
Off-grid commune founded 1978 after the Nambassa festival; peaked at ~120 residents on collectively-held land, reachable only by off-road track.
Why
Born at Nambassa festival from the belief that land ownership is the root of humanity's problems — so they bought a valley and set it free: nobody owns it, forever.
Status
Barely alive: population fell to ~20, abandoned homes, empty communal cookhouse. Documented in The Last Man of Mahana.
Review
The purest test of the anti-ownership idea, and its slow decay is the verdict: removing ownership also removed responsibility, and the open-door principle left the community with no way to keep out people who came to exploit it. The kids left for the city anyway. Beautiful, sad, and the perfect opening story — a utopia that failed not with a bang but with a quiet cookhouse.

Kowloon Walled CityHong Kong

GoneWHY-RANK 002/219
SURVIVAL20
REASON INTEREST100
BRING-YOUR-OWN-VISION95
HOMOPHILY5
About
A lawless enclave where 33,000+ people packed into one solid block of interlocked buildings with no architects, no regulation, no government.
Why
No one founded it — a treaty loophole left one block outside all law, and 33,000 people built a working city in the void. The control group for every planned utopia.
Status
Demolished 1994; now a park.
Review
The inverse control group for every planned utopia on this list: zero design, and yet water pipes, postal routes, rooftop schools and hundreds of unlicensed dentists all functioned. The strongest evidence that order emerges without planners — and, in the same breath, the strongest evidence you wouldn't want to live in what emerges. Horrifying and miraculous at once; footage and photos are plentiful.

Nueva GermaniaParaguay

GoneWHY-RANK 003/219
SURVIVAL30
REASON INTEREST99
BRING-YOUR-OWN-VISION2
HOMOPHILY100
About
A colony founded 1887 by Nietzsche's sister and her antisemitic husband to establish a "pure" German settlement in the jungle.
Why
Elisabeth Förster-Nietzsche and her antisemite husband set out to breed a 'pure' Aryan Germany in the Paraguayan jungle. Nietzsche despised it; the jungle and intermarriage defeated it.
Status
Failed almost immediately (the founder took poison); descendants remain, mostly intermarried with Paraguayans.
Review
History's tidiest irony: the racist utopia was defeated by its own premise. The land resisted, the crops failed, the founder killed himself — and survival required exactly the intermarriage the colony was created to prevent. The descendants living happily as ordinary Paraguayans are the complete refutation, written in genealogy. Ideology met jungle; jungle won.

Oneida CommunityNew York, USA

GoneWHY-RANK 005/219
SURVIVAL35
REASON INTEREST98
BRING-YOUR-OWN-VISION5
HOMOPHILY90
About
1848–1881 religious commune practising total sharing of property, work and relationships ("complex marriage").
Why
Noyes preached that perfection was possible now — so sin's institutions had to go, including marriage, replaced by 'complex marriage' of every member to every member.
Status
Dissolved 1881; survives only as the Oneida silverware company.
Review
Thirty years of the most radical social experiment in American history, held together entirely by one man's theology — and dissolved within years of his decline. Communes that abolish the family need something stronger than the family to replace it, and charisma has a lifespan. That it ended as a cutlery brand is the darkly funny footnote: the commune died, the company thrived. Capitalism got the last word.

GoneWHY-RANK 007/219
SURVIVAL12

BRING-YOUR-OWN-VISION2
HOMOPHILY30
About
Henry Ford's 1928 American-style town in the jungle — square dances, alcohol bans and cafeteria hamburgers imposed on Brazilian rubber workers.
Why
Ford needed rubber but wanted more: to export Michigan itself — square dances, prohibition, punch-clocks — into the Amazon. The jungle and the workers both revolted.
Status
Abandoned by Ford in 1945; a small Brazilian town lives among the ruins.
Review
Failure on every axis at once: botanical (rubber monoculture invited blight the plantation never beat), social (workers rioted over the food and the timeclocks), and personal (Ford never once visited). The fantasy that you can control people like production lines met the jungle and lost. The ruins in the rainforest are the best visual metaphor available for the whole "controlled utopia" category.

A multiracial socialist congregation genuinely fleeing American racism to build equality in the jungle — the founding reason that makes the ending unbearable.

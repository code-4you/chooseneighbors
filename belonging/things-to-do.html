A place to walk into where people are. Performances, meals, talks, projects, work you can pick up any time — and an app that quietly knows what's on and who's around. So that waking at 3am with nothing and no one is never the shape of your life.

The opposite of isolation isn't a busy calendar. It's somewhere to go where you already belong.

Most of us fight loneliness by trying to schedule it away — texting a friend, planning a night out, booking a holiday. It works for a few hours, then the quiet returns. The problem isn't a lack of effort; it's that there's nowhere that's simply there, no place you can drift into without a plan, no shared life happening down the path.

Belonging builds that place, and gives it a nervous system: a hall where things are always happening, and an app that connects you to them and to each other.

The meeting place

A hall that's neither home nor work — somewhere you can just be.

At the heart of each village is a shared place you can walk into with no reason and no money needed. It's the "third place" that ordinary suburbs quietly designed away — and it's where a community's daily life actually happens.

Eat together

A buffet of dishes made by the people around you, named and priced by whoever cooked them — the most reliable way humans have ever found to gather.

Something on tonight

A rolling schedule of performances, talks, discussions, music, and services — and you might be on it. Some nights, people are paid to put on something good.

Walls you can edit

Projected, shared screens anyone can put work onto, voted up if people want to see more of it — a living noticeboard for the place.

Room to begin things

Spaces for people's small businesses, a shop for local makers, apartments for newcomers to stay, and work to pick up any time.

The app

It answers one question, well: what is there to do — and who's up for it — right now?

The app isn't a feed to scroll. It's a way to surface the things and people already around you: what's on at the hall, which friends are free, what work or projects you could join, and the interests that connect you to others.

A "things to do" screen

The first thing you see: what's happening near you now, and what your friends are up to — so there's always a next step out of the quiet.

Activities to join

Mark an activity available, active, or hidden. See friends' open activities and nearby groups within a distance you set.

Matched by interest

List what you care about; the app groups you with others who share it, so the right people find the right project.

Work, any time

Switch yourself to available, and pick up tasks or jobs whenever it suits — with short videos showing what each task involves.

The thought behind "#around me"

Wake at an odd hour with that familiar nothing, and the things around you — the hall, the open work, the people nearby — are already sitting quietly in your awareness. Maybe you don't want to see anyone you know that night, and that's fine; you stay invisible and order in, or go somewhere quiet. But these things rest in the back of the mind as a standing answer to isolation — a sense that there's always somewhere, and someone, if you want it.

None of this is forced. You can stay home, stay invisible, do nothing at all. The point isn't to fill your time — it's that the option to belong is always there, a few steps down the path, on a night you didn't plan. That's what turns a set of homes into a place that's alive.

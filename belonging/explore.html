Explore Belonging

An affordable community where you choose your neighbours.

Belonging is a new kind of community designed around what actually makes a life good: people you chose, a home you own instead of rent, and a real say over where you live. Built so that no one has to live, or grow old, in isolation. Start with the short film, then explore every part below.

A 55-second introduction.

The idea

The four things Belonging gives you — the core of how it works.

Choose your neighbours →

The mechanic the whole community is named for: apply to live near the people you want around you, with open space always kept free so friends can move in and so you can move freely. How it differs from cohousing, co-ops, and intentional communities.

/choose-your-neighbours.html
Homes you own, never rent →

Affordable home ownership from day one — from a free starter shell to full ownership — kept affordable forever by holding the land in a community land trust so it can't be speculated on. The full affordability model explained.

/homes.html
Always something to do →

The shared hall and the app that keep community life alive — meals, performances, projects, and work you can pick up any time — so isolation is never the default. Designed social spaces that turn neighbours into community.

/things-to-do.html
You design it →

Self-government for residents: self-nominating organisers, an open index of community ideas, and funding for the ones that make the place better. The most local layer of government, owned by the people who live there.

/you-design-it.html

What makes it last

Six design principles — grounded in research and real communities — that keep a chosen community from falling apart. Read the overview.

A · Governing ourselves →

Resolving conflict without exile, an anti-capture charter, consent-based decisions, and transparent money — so the community doesn't turn on its members or get captured from inside.

/governing-ourselves.html
B · Pulling our weight →

Contribution without coercion through time banking, care built in as infrastructure, and real support pathways — answering who does the necessary work, and who notices when someone is struggling.

/pulling-our-weight.html
C · Built to last →

Building for families and a mix of generations, keeping villages small enough to know, belonging without a creed, and clear rules for when someone dies, defaults, or leaves.

/built-to-last.html
D · Why it works on people →

The mechanism that turns strangers into neighbours, the social architecture that produces it, and the decades of evidence that connection lengthens and improves life.

/why-it-works-on-people.html
E · Keeping people safe →

Choosing neighbours lawfully on conduct not identity, safeguarding the vulnerable, and an app that protects rather than watches.

/keeping-people-safe.html
F · The practical backbone →

The legal structure, low running costs, economic resilience, and outcome measurement that turn the vision into something investors and councils can rely on.

Design principles

What makes
it last

It's tempting to think a community succeeds if you simply pick good people. It doesn't. Every part of history's communes and co-ops shows the warm parts only survive when a few unglamorous things are handled first.

These six parts are the structure the warmth depends on. Each one answers a specific, well-documented way that good communities fall apart — by turning on their members, being quietly captured, seizing up, ageing out, hardening into a creed, exposing the vulnerable, or running out of money. None of it is bureaucracy for its own sake.

Read them in order, or jump to whatever matters most to you. They cross-reference one another, because they're really one connected design, not six separate essays.

A
Governing ourselves →

How a chosen community handles its own friction — so it doesn't turn on its members, get captured by insiders, or seize up in endless argument.

Covers: resolving conflict without exile · a charter that can't be captured · deciding by consent · money everyone can see
B
Pulling our weight →

If homes are cheap or free and work is optional, who does the necessary work — and who notices when someone is struggling? The answer can't be good will alone.

Covers: contribution without coercion (time banking) · care as infrastructure · real support, not just good vibes
C
Built to last →

How the community outlives the people who start it — by renewing itself, staying small enough to know, holding people without binding their minds, and meeting hardship with dignity.

Covers: building for families & generations · small villages, not one big town · belonging without a creed · when someone dies, defaults, or leaves
D
Why it works on people →

The mechanism that turns strangers into neighbours, the design that produces it on purpose, and the decades of hard evidence that connection makes life longer and healthier.

Covers: chosen, not forced · designing the accidental hello · the health & longevity evidence
E
Keeping people safe →

Every strength here has a shadow. Choosing neighbours can tip into exclusion; the freedom to move can leave the vulnerable unsupported; the app can be turned against people. Designed with care, none of them do.

Covers: choosing neighbours without discriminating · a home you can actually leave · an app that protects, not watches
F
The practical backbone →

The unglamorous part a serious backer turns to first — the legal skeleton, the running costs, the resilience, and the honest measurement that lets the project keep its promises.

Covers: the legal structure · low bills, not an ideology · economic resilience · measuring whether it works

A note on how this was made

These design principles were developed with AI, working from the ideas in the book and from established research and real-world models — then checked, shaped, and owned by the people who'll actually live them. The thinking is offered openly; the choices remain ours.
